Keep your patient's pH in range.

Each run starts with a real-style patient. Acid (H⁺) and base (HCO₃⁻) are flooding their blood. Stop them before they reach the pH zone, then read the ABG and name the problem.

  1. Tap an ion to send it to the lungs or kidneys.
  2. Lungs (A) are sprinters: they breathe off acid as CO2, fast, but can't remove base.
  3. Kidneys (B) are slow but strong: when charged, they filter a batch of acid and base.
  4. Last 30 seconds. A pH of 6.80 or 7.80 ends the run.
  5. Listen: a calm chime means pH is in range. A heartbeat means trouble, and it speeds up the further pH drifts.
  6. After the run, read the ABG and pick the diagnosis.

The real science: normal arterial blood pH is 7.35 to 7.45. Within minutes your lungs adjust acid by breathing off more or less CO2, and over hours to days your kidneys excrete H⁺ and keep or release bicarbonate. The full VR game lets you play as both.
